
# EXERCISE 2 – DISTANCE AS THE LENGTH OF A VECTOR

## Given

The points are:

$$
A=(1,2,3)
$$

$$
B=(4,-2,6)
$$

We need to:

1. Construct the vector AB.
2. Compute its length.
3. Compute the distance between the points using the coordinate formula.
4. Explain why both calculations give the same result.

## Solution

### Step 1 – Construct the Vector AB

To find the vector from point A to point B, subtract the coordinates of A from the coordinates of B.

$$
\overrightarrow{AB}=B-A
$$

Substitute the coordinates:

$$
\overrightarrow{AB}=(4-1,-2-2,6-3)
$$

Simplify:

$$
\overrightarrow{AB}=(3,-4,3)
$$

### Step 2 – Calculate the Length of the Vector

The length of a vector in three-dimensional space is calculated using the formula:

$$
|v|=\sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2}
$$

For the vector:

$$
\overrightarrow{AB}=(3,-4,3)
$$

Substitute the components:

$$
|\overrightarrow{AB}|=\sqrt{3^2+(-4)^2+3^2}
$$

Calculate the squares:

$$
|\overrightarrow{AB}|=\sqrt{9+16+9}
$$

Simplify:

$$
|\overrightarrow{AB}|=\sqrt{34}
$$

Therefore, the length of the vector is:

$$
|\overrightarrow{AB}|=\sqrt{34}
$$

### Step 3 – Calculate the Distance Using the Coordinate Formula

The distance between two points in three-dimensional space is:

$$
d(A,B)=\sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2+(y_2-y_1)^2+(z_2-z_1)^2}
$$

Given:

$$
A=(1,2,3)
$$

$$
B=(4,-2,6)
$$

Substitute the coordinates:

$$
d(A,B)=\sqrt{(4-1)^2+(-2-2)^2+(6-3)^2}
$$

Simplify:

$$
d(A,B)=\sqrt{3^2+(-4)^2+3^2}
$$

$$
d(A,B)=\sqrt{9+16+9}
$$

Therefore:

$$
d(A,B)=\sqrt{34}
$$

### Step 4 – Compare Both Calculations

Using the vector length method:

$$
|\overrightarrow{AB}|=\sqrt{34}
$$

Using the coordinate distance formula:

$$
d(A,B)=\sqrt{34}
$$

Both methods give exactly the same result.

The reason is that the vector AB represents the displacement from point A to point B.

Its length measures the straight-line distance between these two points.

The coordinate distance formula calculates the same length directly from the differences between the corresponding coordinates.

### Final Answer

The vector from A to B is:

$$
\overrightarrow{AB}=(3,-4,3)
$$

Its length is:

$$
|\overrightarrow{AB}|=\sqrt{34}
$$

The distance between points A and B is:

$$
d(A,B)=\sqrt{34}
$$

### Conclusion

The distance between two points is equal to the length of the vector connecting them.

Both methods use the same coordinate differences and the three-dimensional Pythagorean theorem.

Therefore, the vector length formula and the coordinate distance formula are mathematically equivalent.
